Innovating to make traditional media spaces work harder

Old media, new media

Head of Technology - Andy Buchan · 19 November 2021

Augmented reality isn't anything new but as an agency we're always looking for ways (old and new) to leverage technology and activate brands in more interesting ways. One of the core challenges that existed for AR was the lack of audience. In order for brands to launch an AR experience they were required to create their own mobile application and distribute it. Both significant challenges to launching tactical experiences.

Instagram (well Facebook) to the rescue! Over the past 12 months both platforms have rolled and evolved a series of Augmented Reality features that allow to do more than just face filters and bunny ears. These extensions to Facebook messenger and Instagram now give brands an audience instantly at no cost and without any of the friction attached to downloading an app. Given that in the UK alone Instagram has 25 million active users we can step over all of the previous logistical challenges.

So what does it mean when any brand can deploy an AR experience to a huge audience with little media spend? Well part of what we do here at LOAF and in our Emotion Lab is to think about meaningful applications for technology. Not technology for technologies sake. So no, we're not launching more bunny ears of dog face filters (sorry). We apply and blend our technical understanding with our experience methodology to define opportunities and establish the potential for brands.

With that in mind we applied our thinking towards one of our favourite clients, Bruntwood, who are one of the UK's largest property developers. One of their largest developments, right at the heart of Manchester is Circle Square - a beautiful new mixed use neighbourhood doing extraordinary in and around the city. We worked with bruntwood on the place making of this new district and destination

Bringing to life the core proposition of ‘extraordinary taking shape’, we wanted to test out these new AR abilities through instagram - so we set out to bring to life elements of a hoarding design we had done a few months previous.

What we learned

A hoarding may not be the perfect place the the use of AR, at least not in this location. Circle square is based right in the middle of Manchester’s Oxford road, a road with a heavy footfall and also parallel to a main road. However, the ability to engage further with customers, through platforms they use everyday is mightily exciting, and begins to shift what can be done when the barriers for engagement are reduced.

Movement, animation and textures takes AR to the next level - and we will be experimenting further soon.
