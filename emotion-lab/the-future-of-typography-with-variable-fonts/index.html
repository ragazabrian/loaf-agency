The future of typography with variable fonts

Creativity and accessibility

Zak Rains – Creative · 10 October 2021

“Typography is the craft of endowing human language with a durable visual form.”
? Robert Bringhurst, The Elements of Typographic Style

Often the craft of typography sits in conflict with its durability. As media evolves, type must move with it to make sure it’s fit for purpose. Thankfully, technology is catching up to empower designers to satisfy their creative desires without abandoning the evolving accessibility and responsive demands of modern design. Variable font technology enables brands to tweak their font to improve customer touch points throughout their brand experience — whether that is accessibility, expression or immersive digital experiences.

A variable font is a font that behaves like several fonts, in one single file rather than multiple files. Presently, when a user visits a web page, each individual font file has to be downloaded to show the text. Variable fonts are an evolving technology that offer creative control, with great benefits to accessibility, usability, and flexibility.

Accessible design is good design. With an ageing population and evolving technology, there is no reason not to design for all. Everyone should have access to good design. With variable fonts, type can be adjusted to increase legibility or adjusted to its environment. The changing conditions around the user (low light, for example) could prompt a subtle shift in weight with significant benefits. As people spend more and more time in front of screens, brands should work to make their experience as palatable as possible. One size fits all typography doesn’t work for brands and it doesn’t work for consumers. These benefits to accessibility are just the beginning. There is so much potential for immersive, expressive, and responsive typography.

One of the biggest trends for 2020 is a strong move towards type-only creative solutions. As designers innovate with bold, decorative type, they are pushing the boundaries of modern design. But even type purists can admit that poin`t size carries weight. Iconic designer Massimo Vignelli once said “You can say, "I love you," in Helvetica. And you can say it with Helvetica Extra Light if you want to be really fancy. Or you can say it with the Extra Bold if it's really intensive and passionate.” With variable fonts, you could personalise the experience and prompt an emotional response of your audience. A signature weight of your brand’s font offers another level of distinctiveness.

There are effectiveness benefits to a type-only approach as well. Single minded statements are memorable and efficient. Pair that with the creative potential of variable fonts, there’s no reason to let this trend pass us by. Interactive digital experiences, apps, and gaming offer opportunities as well.

Variable fonts allow you to control your brand experience and increases brand equity, recognisability, and better user experience. They are still an early technology so support for it is limited but the potential is there. The impact on brand experience alone is enough for us to pay attention. Watch this space because the future fonts are coming.
