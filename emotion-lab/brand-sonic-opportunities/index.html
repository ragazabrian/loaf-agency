Why brands need to listen

The power of sound

Creative Director / Co-founder – Dave Mullen · 25 August 2021

Music plays a huge part in my life. Whether that’s releasing some energy on the drums, plugging in on my commute or tuning out on the living room sofa – music inspires me. It changes my mood, it makes me feel alive. The power of music is inescapable – it’s one of the biggest channels of entertainment in the world, and a key aspect of virtually every other one. Film wouldn’t be film without music. Neither would gaming. Music is an expression of emotion for its composer, creating an emotional and cultural connection with any listener.

But what about the power of sound – the elemental foundation of music? Sound has been used to heal for thousands of years. It’s used in other positive and therapeutic ways all over the world, and its benefits to health and wellbeing are truly exciting.

But it’s a power that’s still largely invisible in the world of brand. It’s under-exploited and under-explored, and essentially non-existent in many brand planning documents. Sound design and sonic branding aren’t new, but with a new wave of research and development in the field, more brands are beginning to realise how sound can have a profound effect across countless touchpoints.

Sound can rouse emotions and connect in ways the other senses can’t. From Skype re-designing their 'loader sound' to make the app feel quicker and more agile to ‘The sound of HSBC’, an entire sonic brand developed by the bank in collaboration with musician Jean-Michel Jarre, brands are utilising sound more and more. And they’re working harder to understand how they can harness all that power.

Going deeper

Since sound is vital to our work in honing brand experiences, we’re looking at it more closely than ever at present, especially the ways that different sounds and frequencies effect mood and emotions.

Most recently, our whole team embraced a ’Sonic Healing’ session, designed by Neil Davis, Director of City Therapy and active proponent of the power of sound. Neil has been delivering sonic experiences all over the world for the past five years, for names such as Above & Beyond. His approach is based on re-aligning the vibrations that emanate from every atom in every molecule in your body, and the experience was as surprising as it was enjoyable.

I had never experienced sound in this raw, focused way before. With nothing to look at, and no triggers nudging me to ‘feel’ something specific, the sense was elevated and incredibly powerful. From the sound of Tibetan singing bowls and osteophonic tuning forks, to native flutes, buffalo, ocean drums and electronic beats, the result was a cascading journey of emotions and feelings. Emerging from the session we all agreed that the feeling was akin to meditation, albeit with a sense of relaxation and fresh focus.

As different as the experience was, it certainly reflected my own personal connection with music and sound, and the essential truth that human beings have utilised for centuries – that there is something unique about the way sound impacts us. With the explosion of voice technology and the speedy development of utilities across most sectors, it’s clear that defining what a brand sounds like should now be as important as defining how it looks and behaves. 

Sound matters. It powerfully affects customer experience, attitudes and behaviour. It shapes moods and – more importantly – can help define strong emotional connections about a brand, product and service choice.

So if you’ve not started to develop your brands sonic strategy, and how your brand sounds, now might be a good time to start.
