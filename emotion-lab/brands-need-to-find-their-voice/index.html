Brands need to find their voice

Better brand experiences through voice

Innovation Director – Tom Downing · 27 September 2020

There is so much hype around voice assistants. Duly so given the opportunities, but I cannot help think brands are missing a trick when it comes to making real emotive, purpose-driven voice experiences.

Alexa and Google have one voice. You can distinguish your skill by adding hints of personality in the form of polys, but really, we all hear and picture one personality. Too often I see technology used by brands with a pure tech-driven motive. In order to be truly effective when it comes to consumer engagement, the story has to start from the brand's personality.

During the launch of a new Voice group Voice2, founded by VCCP’s Innovation Lead Will Harvey and ex head of Emerging Platforms & Partnerships team at Lego James Poulter, conversations were about just this. For brands to take voice seriously, they need to look at sonic branding and factor it into their wider long-term brand development. A brand’s voice is just that, a piece of identity it should own and be recognised for. What does Ford sound like compared to Honda? 

The real trick is to start with a brand experience perspective. Find the brand narrative that intertwines with a valuable voice experience idea. Get experts who live and breathe brand tone of voice - copywriters, brand strategists AND technologists in one room. These are the people that should be exploring the early stages of a brands voice skill project. Work out how to take your audience on a journey that feels like it’s relevant to your brand.  

This is the process we take via our Emotion Lab, a discovery space for us to collaborate with brands to explore what it means to innovate outside the realms of normal marketing comms restraints. The adoption rate of voice assistants has overtaken the uptake of smartphones. This alone shows how as a society worldwide, we want to use our voice more and more. We, as brands leaders, just have to work out our role in all this.

The future is so exciting for voice with untapped opportunities yet to be explored.
